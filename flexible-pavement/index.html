Flexible Pavement Design for Dublin’s Variable Subgrades

Site investigations you can build on.

LEARN MORE

The difference in ground conditions between a site in Tallaght and one along the River Liffey quays can be stark. Tallaght often sits on stiff Dublin boulder clay that drains reasonably well once the upper weathered layer is stripped, while the quays area hides lenses of soft alluvium and made ground with groundwater barely a metre below the road formation. A pavement section that works beautifully on the till might rut within three years on the alluvium if the capping layer and subbase aren’t adjusted. In our experience doing flexible pavement design across Dublin, the most cost-sensitive decision is not the binder course thickness but how you treat the upper 600 mm of the subgrade. We routinely cross-check layer moduli with CBR testing for roads before locking in a design, and when the formation is suspect we recommend a quick test pit investigation to confirm the actual soil profile rather than relying solely on borehole logs.

A flexible pavement designed for Dublin boulder clay can last twice as long as one specified generically—the difference is in the capping layer and drainage detailing.

Process overview

A recent industrial estate expansion near Ballycoolin illustrated the classic Dublin challenge. The site investigation showed a stiff brown boulder clay with average CBR values around 8%, but three isolated pockets of soft grey silt within the footprint—remnants of a post-glacial pond that had been infilled and forgotten. The contractor wanted a uniform 450 mm pavement section across the entire yard. Instead, we designed a reinforced flexible pavement with a 200 mm asphalt surfacing and bound base over a 300 mm cement-stabilised capping, but only within the soft pocket footprints; the rest of the yard stayed on a traditional granular base and 120 mm asphalt. This selective approach saved approximately 1,200 square metres of unnecessary stabilisation. In Dublin, the boulder clay itself is a decent subgrade once compacted at optimum moisture, and combining the pavement design with a Proctor compaction specification ensures the formation achieves the stiffness assumed in the analytical model. On larger schemes where deep asphalt strength is warranted, we adapt the design to the long-life pavement approach, keeping tensile strains below 70 microstrain to avoid bottom-up fatigue cracking over a 40-year horizon.

Local context

The Dublin boulder clay is a lodgement till that can be deceptively variable. In its natural dense state, undrained shear strengths exceed 150 kPa and it makes for excellent pavement support. But where it has been weathered, disturbed, or exposed to prolonged wetting, the upper metre can soften to a CBR of 2-3% and become highly moisture-sensitive. The biggest risk we see in Dublin flexible pavement design is not the traffic loading but the seasonal fluctuation of the water table within the formation depth. A pavement that performs well during an August site visit can be near failure by February if free-draining subbase is omitted and the formation is not cross-fall drained. Where the pavement alignment passes through cut-to-fill transitions, differential stiffness can cause longitudinal cracking within the first two years unless the fill is compacted to the same modulus target as the cut.

Quick answers

What is the typical cost range for a flexible pavement design package on a Dublin commercial development?

For a standalone flexible pavement design report covering subgrade investigation, layer thickness optimisation, and specification drafting, budgets in Dublin generally run from €1,400 to €4,360 depending on the number of traffic loading classes and whether we need to analyse multiple pavement sections across cut-fill transitions.

How do you determine whether a site needs stabilised capping or can use conventional granular capping?

We run a series of laboratory soaked CBR tests on remoulded subgrade samples at the expected field density and moisture content. If the soaked CBR is consistently below 5%, cement or lime stabilisation is usually warranted. Between 5% and 8% we assess traffic sensitivity; above 8% a well-compacted granular capping layer generally suffices.

How long does a flexible pavement design take from site investigation to final report?

Once the laboratory CBR and classification results are available—usually two weeks after sampling—the analytical design and reporting phase takes five to seven working days for a single pavement section. Multiple sections or complex geometry may add another week.
