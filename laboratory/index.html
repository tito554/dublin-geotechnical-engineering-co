Laboratory in Dublin

Site investigations you can build on.

Geotechnical laboratory testing in Dublin supports the complete characterisation of soils and weak rock encountered across the city and its commuter belt, from the glacial tills and fluvioglacial gravels of the Dublin Basin to the alluvial deposits along the Liffey and Tolka estuaries. Our accredited facility provides physical and mechanical testing programmes that align with the requirements of Irish Standard I.S. EN 1997-2:2007, the National Annex for Eurocode 7, and relevant guidance from the Institution of Engineers of Ireland. Routine index testing such as grain size analysis by wet and dry sieving and sedimentation informs the classification of cohesive tills and granular deposits, while Atterberg limits determine the liquid and plastic limits of Dublin’s characteristic lodgement tills and laminated clays, directly feeding into the assessment of shrink-swell potential and construction suitability.

Methodology follows the procedures detailed in I.S. EN ISO 17892, the harmonised series for geotechnical laboratory testing, with strict adherence to the quality management framework of ISO 17025. Particle size distribution is determined in accordance with I.S. EN ISO 17892-4, with hydrometer analysis applied to the silt and clay fraction below 63 µm where cohesive strata such as the Brown Boulder Clay or Black Shale-derived soils are encountered. Atterberg limit determinations comply with I.S. EN ISO 17892-12, using the Casagrande cup and cone penetrometer methods to define consistency indices critical for earthworks specification. For projects requiring strength and stiffness parameters, the triaxial test programme is executed under consolidated drained or consolidated undrained conditions per I.S. EN ISO 17892-9, with pore pressure measurement and effective stress paths interpreted to deliver drained and undrained shear strength parameters for foundation design and slope stability analysis in Dublin’s overconsolidated tills.

Typical project applications in Dublin include residential and commercial basement excavations in the city centre, where retaining wall design demands reliable effective stress parameters from triaxial testing of the underlying limestone till. Infrastructure schemes such as the MetroLink corridor and DART+ expansion require systematic classification and strength testing of mixed-face conditions along tunnel alignments, combining grain size analysis of granular horizons with triaxial tests on cohesive units to define ground behaviour. Low-rise and medium-rise residential developments on greenfield sites in Fingal and South Dublin rely on Atterberg limit determinations and particle size distribution to verify compliance with the Department of Housing’s Specification for Road Works and to confirm suitability of on-site soils for reuse. Wind farm and data centre projects on the periphery of the Greater Dublin Area also demand rigorous grain size analysis for access road and foundation design where variable glacial deposits dominate.

The testing process begins with sample receipt and logging in accordance with I.S. EN ISO 22475-1, followed by specimen preparation under controlled humidity and temperature conditions to preserve natural moisture content. Clients receive a comprehensive geotechnical laboratory report containing individual test results, graphical plots including particle size distribution curves, plasticity charts, and stress-strain and Mohr-Coulomb failure envelopes from triaxial testing, all cross-referenced to borehole logs and field descriptions. Turnaround times are managed to align with project programme milestones, and all data is delivered in formats compatible with geotechnical interpretive reporting and BIM workflows. This integrated laboratory capability provides Dublin’s consulting engineers and contractors with a single-source solution for defensible design parameters, reducing geological uncertainty and supporting efficient foundation and earthworks design across the capital’s varied ground conditions.

Quick answers

What is the typical cost range for anchor design and testing in Dublin?

Anchor design and testing services in Dublin typically range from €870 for a straightforward single-anchor verification to €3,680 for a comprehensive design package covering multiple anchor types, corrosion protection detailing, and supervised acceptance testing on a medium-sized excavation project.

How does Dublin Boulder Clay affect anchor bond stress values?

Dublin Boulder Clay is an overconsolidated lodgement till with a stiff to hard consistency. In intact till, ultimate bond stresses of 300–450 kPa are achievable with post-grouting techniques. However, the till contains discontinuous sand and gravel lenses — often only 100–300 mm thick — where bond stress can drop below 100 kPa. Each anchor zone must be geologically logged during drilling to confirm the design bond length falls entirely within competent till.

What corrosion protection level is required for permanent anchors in Dublin?

Permanent anchors in Dublin require double corrosion protection (Class II per I.S. EN 1537) due to the urban environment and presence of the LUAS tram system, which introduces stray current corrosion risk. The protection system must include a corrugated plastic sheath over the tendon, cement grout as primary barrier, and a smooth outer sheath with end caps. Electrical isolation testing should be performed on each anchor to verify integrity before lock-off.
