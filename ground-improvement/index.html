Improvement in Dublin

Site investigations you can build on.

LEARN MORE

Improvement in Dublin addresses the engineering challenges posed by the region's complex glacial and post-glacial geology, where soft alluvial silts, peats, and loose sands frequently underlie urban and industrial development sites. The overarching goal of these techniques is to enhance bearing capacity, mitigate total and differential settlement, and remediate liquefaction potential in granular soils, ensuring compliance with the stringent performance criteria of Eurocode 7 and the Irish National Annex, as reinforced by the Institution of Engineers of Ireland. Our specialist services encompass targeted solutions such as permeation and compaction grouting for fine-grained soils and deep vibrocompaction for granular deposits, each selected based on a rigorous site-specific ground model derived from comprehensive site investigations conforming to IS EN 1997-2.

The methodological framework for any Improvement project in Ireland is governed by a design-based approach that integrates field testing with execution standards detailed in BS EN 14731 for deep vibration and BS EN 12715 for grouting operations. For problematic, compressible cohesive soils and mixed fills prevalent in the Dublin basin, the installation of vibro-replacement stone columns is a primary solution, constructing stiff, high-modulus inclusions that composite with the native soil to support conventional shallow foundations. This technique is meticulously validated through pre- and post-treatment In-Situ, including Cone Penetration Tests (CPT) and load tests, to confirm the achieved undrained shear strength and stiffness parameters meet the specified design values in accordance with the Irish Building Regulations Technical Guidance Document A.

Typical applications across the Dublin metropolitan area involve the enabling of critical infrastructure and commercial developments on marginal lands, such as the docklands reclamations and industrial estates in Park West. A characteristic project involves the deployment of vibrocompaction to densify loose hydraulic and estuarine sands beneath proposed warehouse slabs, effectively eliminating the risk of seismic-induced liquefaction as per the hazard mapping for eastern Ireland. Similarly, the construction of multi-storey residential blocks over deep deposits of soft, lacustrine clay is routinely facilitated by the installation of stone columns, which transfer structural loads through the weak stratum to a competent bearing layer, preventing excessive settlement that would compromise serviceability.

Our process commences with a constructability review of the ground investigation data, leading to a detailed method statement and a comprehensive site-specific trial to calibrate installation parameters and confirm performance targets. The primary deliverable is a technically verified, improved ground mass with certified design parameters, accompanied by a full quality control record of installation logs and post-treatment verification test results, providing a robust, risk-mitigated foundation solution. By combining deep local geological knowledge with specialist execution, we provide a value-engineered alternative to deep piling, consistently delivering a programme-certain and cost-effective foundation strategy that accelerates site readiness for principal contractors and developers in the competitive Dublin construction market.

Quick answers

What is the typical cost range for anchor design and testing in Dublin?

Anchor design and testing services in Dublin typically range from €870 for a straightforward single-anchor verification to €3,680 for a comprehensive design package covering multiple anchor types, corrosion protection detailing, and supervised acceptance testing on a medium-sized excavation project.

How does Dublin Boulder Clay affect anchor bond stress values?

Dublin Boulder Clay is an overconsolidated lodgement till with a stiff to hard consistency. In intact till, ultimate bond stresses of 300–450 kPa are achievable with post-grouting techniques. However, the till contains discontinuous sand and gravel lenses — often only 100–300 mm thick — where bond stress can drop below 100 kPa. Each anchor zone must be geologically logged during drilling to confirm the design bond length falls entirely within competent till.

What corrosion protection level is required for permanent anchors in Dublin?

Permanent anchors in Dublin require double corrosion protection (Class II per I.S. EN 1537) due to the urban environment and presence of the LUAS tram system, which introduces stray current corrosion risk. The protection system must include a corrugated plastic sheath over the tendon, cement grout as primary barrier, and a smooth outer sheath with end caps. Electrical isolation testing should be performed on each anchor to verify integrity before lock-off.
