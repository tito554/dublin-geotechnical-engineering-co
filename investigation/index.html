Investigation in Dublin

Site investigations you can build on.

LEARN MORE

Ground investigation in Dublin is the essential first stage of any construction or civil engineering project, designed to characterise subsurface conditions and mitigate ground-related risk. The city’s geology is dominated by glacial tills overlying Carboniferous limestone and shale bedrock, with frequent transitions between stiff boulder clay and weathered rock, particularly along the coastal belt and river valleys. Compliance with the Irish National Annex to Eurocode 7, including I.S. EN 1997-2:2007, and the requirements of the Building Control (Amendment) Regulations 2014 are mandatory for all site investigations. In practice, this means designing a phased investigation that combines techniques such as SPT Drilling and CPT Test to deliver the geotechnical parameters required for foundation design and earthworks assessment.

Methodology in Ireland follows the guidance set out in the Institution of Engineers of Ireland’s *Specification for Ground Investigation* and the UK’s BS 5930, which remains the primary procedural standard adopted locally. Boreholes are typically advanced using cable percussion or rotary drilling rigs, with Standard Penetration Tests performed at regular intervals in accordance with I.S. EN ISO 22476-3. Where stratigraphic continuity and rapid in-situ profiling are required, static Cone Penetration Tests (CPTu) provide near-continuous data on soil behaviour type, undrained shear strength, and pore pressure response—particularly valuable in Dublin’s soft alluvial deposits and made ground. Supplementary Test Pits are frequently employed at shallow depth to expose fill materials, verify buried service corridors, and allow undisturbed sampling of cohesive soils. All fieldwork is supervised by a chartered geotechnical engineer, with laboratory testing conducted at INAB-accredited facilities to comply with IS EN ISO 17892 standards.

In Dublin, ground investigation programmes are shaped by the city’s dense urban fabric and its specific development pressures. Typical projects include medium- to high-rise residential schemes on former industrial lands in the docklands, where deep soft estuarine deposits necessitate careful assessment of settlement and pile capacity, and infrastructure upgrades such as the BusConnects corridors and Irish Water drainage projects, where linear investigations must navigate existing utilities and constrained access. For these applications, CPT Test arrays are often deployed to map compressible layers beneath proposed embankments or heavily loaded floor slabs, while targeted SPT boreholes provide the necessary information for retaining wall and deep foundation design in the glacial till.

The investigation process begins with a desk study and walkover survey, followed by mobilisation of track-mounted or restricted-access rigs suited to Dublin’s often tight city-centre sites. Fieldwork is executed under strict health and safety protocols aligned with the Safety, Health and Welfare at Work (Construction) Regulations 2013. Upon completion, the client receives a comprehensive Ground Investigation Report including factual logs, laboratory test results, and a geotechnical interpretive section that delivers characteristic ground parameters, foundation recommendations, and a contamination screening assessment where required. The result is a defensible, code-compliant ground model that enables structural engineers and architects to proceed with confidence, reducing the risk of unforeseen ground conditions and programme overruns.

Quick answers

What is the typical cost range for anchor design and testing in Dublin?

Anchor design and testing services in Dublin typically range from €870 for a straightforward single-anchor verification to €3,680 for a comprehensive design package covering multiple anchor types, corrosion protection detailing, and supervised acceptance testing on a medium-sized excavation project.

How does Dublin Boulder Clay affect anchor bond stress values?

Dublin Boulder Clay is an overconsolidated lodgement till with a stiff to hard consistency. In intact till, ultimate bond stresses of 300–450 kPa are achievable with post-grouting techniques. However, the till contains discontinuous sand and gravel lenses — often only 100–300 mm thick — where bond stress can drop below 100 kPa. Each anchor zone must be geologically logged during drilling to confirm the design bond length falls entirely within competent till.

What corrosion protection level is required for permanent anchors in Dublin?

Permanent anchors in Dublin require double corrosion protection (Class II per I.S. EN 1537) due to the urban environment and presence of the LUAS tram system, which introduces stray current corrosion risk. The protection system must include a corrugated plastic sheath over the tendon, cement grout as primary barrier, and a smooth outer sheath with end caps. Electrical isolation testing should be performed on each anchor to verify integrity before lock-off.
